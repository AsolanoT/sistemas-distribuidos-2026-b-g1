# Context Map
## Sales Management System — SynkroTech SAS

**Version:** 1.0
**Date:** August 2026

---

## Purpose

Before deciding on the architecture style (see `/adr-001-architecture.md`), this document identifies the **bounded contexts** of SynkroTech SAS's business in business language — not from database tables — and evaluates, for each one, whether it should become an independent service or remain together with another context.

---

## 1. Identified bounded contexts

### 1.1 Authentication and Users

**What is it:** Management of internal system user identities (SynkroTech SAS employees: administrators, sales representatives, inventory personnel), their credentials, and their roles/permissions.

**Why is it a distinct context:** The concept of "user logging in" has no business relationship with "customer making a purchase". They are two distinct entities with different lifecycles: a user is created when an employee is hired; a customer is created when someone makes their first purchase.

**Independent service?** **Yes.**
**Justification:** It is a cross-cutting context — all other contexts depend on it to authorize operations, but it depends on none. Isolating it prevents security logic from being duplicated or coupled to the Customer domain.

---

### 1.2 Customers

**What is it:** Information about SynkroTech SAS customers: contact data, history of their existence as an entity to associate them with sales.

**Why is it a distinct context:** Its lifecycle and business rules (validate identity document, avoid duplicates, deactivate instead of delete) are its own, and do not depend on how products or sales are managed.

**Independent service?** **Yes.**
**Justification:** Low coupling with Products (they don't share business rules). Its only point of contact with Sales is a simple query ("does this customer exist?"), which is well resolved via API without needing to share a data model.

---

### 1.3 Products and Inventory

**What is it:** Product catalog, categories, and available stock control.

**Why is it a distinct context:** It has its own business rules and is more likely to change frequently (new categories, stock adjustments, new products) than the other contexts, and is consulted at high frequency (each sale needs to verify stock).

**Independent service?** **Yes.**
**Justification:** It is the context with the greatest need for frequent consultation and concurrency control (preventing selling a product without stock). Isolating it allows optimizing or scaling it independently if the catalog continues to grow, without affecting Customers or Sales.

---

### 1.4 Sales (includes Reports)

**What is it:** Record of business transactions (sale and its details), orchestration with Customers and Products at the time of sale, and report generation (daily, monthly, best-selling products) from that same transactional information.

**Is Reports its own context, or part of Sales?**
It was considered treating Reports as a separate context, but **it was decided that Reports is part of the same business context as Sales**, not a separate context:
- Reports are calculated **exclusively** from data already generated by Sales (it does not consume information from Customers or Products directly, only from transactions that Sales already possesses).
- There is no Reports business rule that is independent from Sales rules — it is, in essence, an aggregate view of the same transactional information.
- There is no need to scale Reports differently from Sales: both grow at the same rate (a new sale is, at the same time, new data for reports).

**Independent service?** **Yes** (Sales, including Reports, as a single service).
**Justification:** Sales is the context that most depends on the other two (Customers and Products), so isolating it allows it to be the single orchestration point between them, without Customers or Products needing to know that sales exist.

---

## 2. Context map summary

| Context | Independent Service? | Depends on | Depended on by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Authentication and Users | Yes | — | Customers, Products, Sales (JWT validation) |
| Customers | Yes | Auth (JWT validation) | Sales (existence query) |
| Products and Inventory | Yes | Auth (JWT validation) | Sales (stock and price query) |
| Sales (+ Reports) | Yes | Auth, Customers, Products | — |

**Total: 4 contexts → 4 microservices.** No context was grouped with another except Reports, which was integrated as an internal module of Sales because it is part of the same business context (not a simplification due to repository limits, but a real domain decision).

---

## 3. Criteria used to decide "service vs. together"

For each context, the following was evaluated:

1. **Independent change:** Does this context change with a frequency and for reasons different from the others?
2. **Business coupling:** Does it share business rules or only exchange data through simple queries?
3. **Need to scale differently:** Could this context need more resources or availability than the others?
4. **Data ownership:** Who is the natural owner of this information — can a single responsible context be identified?

The 4 identified contexts meet these criteria independently from each other, which supports the decision to separate them as services (formalized in `adr-001-architecture.md`). Reports, on the other hand, does not meet any of the 4 criteria differently from Sales, so it remains together.
